\section*{Paper 1: A Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm\\
\small{QAOA. E.~Farhi and J.~Goldstone, arXiv:1411.4028 (2014) \quad (\texttt{QAOA.pdf})}}

\begin{summary}
QAOA is a variational quantum algorithm for combinatorial optimization. It alternates between a
cost operator built from the objective function and a mixing operator, for $p$ rounds, with $2p$
angles chosen to maximize the expected value of the objective. The paper studies MaxCut and shows
that the approximation ratio cannot get worse as $p$ grows.
\end{summary}

\paragraph{Connection to my research.}
I like to think about how quantum computers can impact \textbf{optimization theory}, which is
deeply connected to \textbf{machine learning}. Optimization is everywhere in ML, and QAOA is a core
quantum approach to these problems. \smiley{}

\paragraph{What to read.}
The whole paper is 16 pages. Focus on the Introduction and the description of the
algorithm (pp.~1--6, before Section III), Section IV (the ring of
disagrees), and Section V (MaxCut on 3-regular graphs). Skim the rest.

\section*{Paper 2: Quantum Algorithms for Nonlinear PDEs\\
\small{R.~Vogt \emph{et al.}, Phys.\ Rev.\ A \textbf{112}, 052431 (2025) \quad
(\texttt{QuantumPDE.pdf})}}

\begin{summary}
Conservation laws lead to nonlinear PDEs whose solutions can develop shocks. This paper shows how
finite-volume (FV) and weighted essentially nonoscillatory (WENO) shock-capturing methods can be
built into a quantum algorithm for nonlinear PDEs (QPDE), and tests the modified algorithm in
simulation on a smooth problem and on two shock-tube (Riemann) problems with known exact solutions.
\end{summary}

\paragraph{Connection to my research.}
I am interested in how quantum computing may influence \textbf{applied mathematics} in general:
PDEs, optimization, control, uncertainty quantification, and scientific computing. This paper gives
a window into the future of \textbf{quantum scientific computing}. \smiley{}

\paragraph{What to read.}
The abstract, Section I (Introduction), Section II.A (the QPDE algorithm summary), the results of
one verification problem of your choice (Sections III--V), and Section VI (Discussion). You can skip
the details of the WENO reconstruction in Section II.C.

\section*{Paper 3: Binary Optimal Control of Single-Flux-Quantum Pulse Sequences\\
\small{R.~H.~Vogt and N.~A.~Petersson, arXiv:2106.10329 (2022) \quad (\texttt{QubitControl.pdf})}}

\begin{summary}
Single flux quantum (SFQ) control drives a superconducting qubit with a train of identical pulses,
each of which is either on or off. This paper treats the choice of pulse sequence as a binary optimal
control problem constrained by Schr\"odinger's equation, and solves it with a relaxed-gradient
trust-region method. The optimized sequences reach gate fidelities above 99.9\% for the $H$ and $X$
gates while suppressing leakage to higher energy levels.
\end{summary}

\paragraph{Connection to my research.}
I am very interested in achieving more \textbf{stable quantum computation} through improved qubit
control. There is also a \textbf{nascent effort} to explore whether ideas from this paper
(originally for superconducting qubits) can be adapted to RIT's \textbf{photonic quantum computing
testbed} for running QAOA circuits. \smiley{}

\paragraph{What to read.}
The abstract, Section 1 (Introduction), Section 2 (the SFQ optimal control problem), the main idea
of Section 4 (the trust-region method), Section 5.1 (the $H$ gate experiment), and Section 6
(Conclusions). You can skip the gradient derivation in Section 3.

\section*{Paper 4: Optimization by Decoded Quantum Interferometry\\
\small{DQI. S.~P.~Jordan \emph{et al.}, arXiv:2408.08292 (2024) \quad (\texttt{DQI.pdf})}}

\begin{summary}
DQI is a quantum algorithm that uses the quantum Fourier transform to turn combinatorial optimization
problems into decoding problems. It prepares a superposition of Dicke states, encodes the problem
into phases, and then uses a classical decoder to uncompute an auxiliary register, so that
interference amplifies good solutions.
\end{summary}

\paragraph{Connection to my research.}
I am especially interested in exploring how the DQI algorithm can be applied to major, real-world
problems in combinatorial optimization. Even discovering a way to effectively use DQI on just one
high-impact combinatorial optimization task, for instance a specific form of the traveling salesman
problem, would already represent a substantial breakthrough. \smiley{}

\paragraph{What to read (important).}
The DQI paper is \textbf{80 pages} long. You do \textbf{not} need to read all of it (unless you
really want to \smiley{}). Everything below is in the first few pages (roughly pp.~1--5):
\begin{itemize}
    \item The abstract.
    \item The opening of Section 1 (Introduction).
    \item The illustrative max-XORSAT example.
    \item Figure 1 and the explanation of the five steps of the DQI algorithm.
    \item The high-level discussion of why the decoding step (uncomputing $|y\rangle$) reduces to
          syndrome decoding, and when it can be done efficiently.
    \item The ``semicircle law'' intuition (at a conceptual level only).
\end{itemize}
This subset is enough to understand the core idea. \smiley{}
